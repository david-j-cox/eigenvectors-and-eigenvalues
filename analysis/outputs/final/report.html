Technical report · 11 September 2026

Estimating per-individual transition operators in a signalled two-alternative task

Specification of the schedule, the state, the estimator and the evaluation, with results from thirteen participants and 55,380 responses.

Summary

Per-participant linear transition operators were estimated on a three-dimensional state observed in ten-response bins, by ridge regression with temporal holdout. Operators were recoverable for all 13 participants and every one improved on a persistence baseline out of sample (median skill 0.489, Wilcoxon p = 2.4 × 10-4). An operator fitted to the first exposure of a context predicted the second in 48 of 52 comparisons (p = 1.6 × 10-7). Dominant-eigenvector similarity between exposures was 0.593 against a same-operator floor of 0.735 at n = 36 transitions, so that statistic is uninformative at this design's cell size and is not used to support replication.

2Schedule

Each alternative k ∈ {A, B} carries a latent value vk ∈ [0, 1] read directly as the probability that a response on it produces a point. On every response, both alternatives recover and the chosen one is then read and depleted:

(1) vk ← min(1, vk + rk) for both k
(2) P(reinforcement) = vj for the chosen j, before depletion
(3) vj ← vj − d if vj − d > ε, else 0

with d = 0.12, rk = 0.086 for the rich alternative and 0.036 for the lean one (0.061 in the symmetric context), vk(0) = 0.70 and ε = 10−9. The floor at ε matters: without it, repeated addition and subtraction of (1) and (3) leaves residues near 10−16, which are not a different reinforcement probability but do behave very differently when divided into.

Both the recovery in (1) and the depletion in (3) are per response. An earlier version applied (1) per unit time, which makes the schedule a function of response rate: a participant responding at a 527 ms median inter-response time accrues roughly three times the recovery per response of one at 181 ms. Across six pilot participants this produced r = 0.992 between median inter-response time and mean latent value, and r = 0.982 against obtained reinforcement rate. Under (1)–(3) as written, holding the response sequence fixed and varying the inter-response time over 150–700 ms leaves mean latent value invariant to within 10−9.

A changeover delay withholds reinforcement for 200 ms and one response following a switch. The reinforcer is withheld but (3) still applies, so the alternative is worked either way.

3State

Responses are aggregated into non-overlapping bins of B = 10 within context. A bin containing fewer than max(3, B/2) responses is dropped rather than contributing a noisier state than the rest of the series. For bin t with responses indexed s:

(4) xt = ( p̂A(t), r̂(t), ℓ(t) )⊤
(5) p̂A(t) = nt−1 Σs∈t 1[choices = A],  r̂(t) = nt−1 Σs∈t 1[reinforceds]
(6) ℓ(t) = nt−1 Σs∈t log(1 + min(ICIs, 5 s))

The cap in (6) is applied before the logarithm so that rare long pauses — a participant looking away, a tab losing focus — do not dominate the coordinate. Switch rate was a fourth candidate coordinate and is excluded; §6.3 gives the criterion and §7.4 the result.

4Estimator

Four architectures are fitted per participant, all linear in the state:

(7)persistence: x̂t+1 = xt
(8)Ai:  x̂t+1 = Ai xt + ci
(9)DMDc: x̂t+1 = Ai xt + Bi ut + ci
(10)Ai,c:  x̂t+1 = Ai,c xt + ci,c

Predictors are standardized on the training split only. Coefficients solve the ridge problem

(11) (Â, ĉ) = arg min Σt∈train ‖xt+1 − A xt − c‖22 + α‖A‖F2

with α chosen from {0.01, 0.1, 1, 10} on an inner temporal split of the training data, so no held-out observation participates in hyperparameter selection. The outer split is also temporal and within context: the earliest 70% of transitions in each context train, the latest test, subject to at least 15 training and 4 test transitions.

The input ut in (9) contains destination-context indicators with the first context as reference. Latent option values are deliberately excluded from ut: under (1)–(3) they are partly a consequence of the participant's own allocation, and including them would reintroduce the response as if it were an environmental cause.

4.1 Evaluation

Skill is the proportional reduction in held-out mean squared error against persistence:

(12) skill = 1 − MSEmodel / MSEpersistence,  MSE = |test|−1 Σt∈test ‖xt+1 − x̂t+1‖22

Skill = 0 means the operator does no better than assuming the participant continues as they were; skill < 0 means worse. Persistence is the right baseline because behavior in this task is strongly autocorrelated, and a model that merely reproduces that autocorrelation should score zero.

5Eigenanalysis

For each fitted Ai, eigenvalues λ1, …, λ3 are ordered by descending modulus. Define

(13)ρ = maxj |λj|  (spectral radius)
(14)t½ = log(½) / log ρ  bins, for 0 < ρ < 1
(15)T = 2π / |arg λ1|  bins, when λ1 is complex

ρ < 1 means perturbations decay and the system returns to a fixed point; t½ is how many bins that takes to half. A complex λ1 means the return oscillates with period (15) rather than decaying monotonically. Since a complex pair has no single real direction, the dominant vector is taken as the renormalized real part, and the complex flag is reported alongside so such a loading is never read as a pure direction.

Two operators are compared by the absolute cosine between dominant eigenvectors,

(16) s(A, A′) = |⟨w1, w1′⟩| / (‖w1‖ ‖w1′‖)

with the absolute value because eigenvector sign is arbitrary. §6.4 gives the reference distribution this must be read against.

6Measurement criteria

6.1 Noise ratio

A coordinate is usable only if its between-bin variance exceeds the sampling variance of estimating it from B responses. For a rate coordinate v observed in bins with nt responses,

(17) η = Ẽt[ vt(1 − vt) / nt ] / Vart(vt)

the numerator being the mean binomial sampling variance and the denominator the total observed between-bin variance. For the continuous coordinate (6) the numerator is Var(ℓ)/B. Acceptance requires η ≤ 0.6. Note η > 1 is attainable and means the observed variation is smaller than counting noise alone would produce.

6.2 Bin width from the participant

Between-bin variance decomposes into signal and sampling parts,

(18) Varobs(B) = σ2true + V1/B

with V1 the per-response sampling variance — p(1−p) for a rate, the mean within-bin variance for a continuous coordinate. Substituting into (17) and requiring η(B) ≤ η* inverts to a closed form for the smallest admissible bin:

(19) B ≥ (1 − η*) V1 / (η* σ2true)

Every term is estimated from the participant's own responding. If σ2true ≤ 0 the bound diverges, which is the correct answer rather than a failure: the coordinate is sampling noise all the way down for that participant and no bin width recovers it.

Choosing B per participant leaves eigenvectors comparable, since they live in the coordinate space, but not eigenvalues, which are per-bin decays. Rescaling by (14) to a half-life in responses, B · log(½)/log ρ, restores comparability.

6.3 Why switch rate fails (19)

At an observed switch rate of p ≈ 0.08 and B = 10, the bin counts are concentrated on {0, 1, 2}, and V1 = p(1−p) ≈ 0.074 against a σ2true that is small or, for several participants, indistinguishable from zero.

6.4 Same-operator floor for (16)

A cosine of 0.59 is not interpretable without knowing what two estimates of the same operator would give at the same number of transitions. That reference distribution was obtained by simulating from operators fitted to real participants, and at n = 40 transitions it has median 0.735 with a 5th–95th percentile range of [0.087, 0.982]. The width of that interval is the point: at this cell size, (16) has almost no resolving power.

7Results

N = 13 participants × 4,260 responses = 55,380. Sessions ran 17.7–32.4 minutes (mean 22.5). Every session stored every response it recorded, verified by comparing the session record's total against stored rows.

7.1 Allocation

7.2 Operator recovery

All 13 participants met the transition minima of §4 and yielded a fitted Ai. Skill (12) was positive for 13 of 13, median 0.489, IQR [0.423, 0.540]; Wilcoxon signed-rank W = 0, p = 2.4 × 10-4. Spectral radii (13) ranged 0.115–0.777, so every operator is contractive; 5 of 13 had complex λ1.

7.3 Replication across exposures

Each color × contingency cell is presented twice, 800 responses apart in the ABAB sequence, giving 36 transitions per estimate at B = 10. Fitting on the first exposure and predicting the second gave positive skill in 48 of 52 comparisons (92%), median 0.503, Wilcoxon W = 114, p = 1.6 × 10-7.

Equation (16) is uninformative at n = 36. Median observed similarity was 0.593, IQR [0.217, 0.787]. The same-operator floor of §6.4 at n = 40 is 0.735 [0.087, 0.982]. The observation lies below that median and deep inside that interval, so it separates nothing — neither supporting nor refuting replication. The held-out prediction result above tests the same hypothesis with far greater power and is what the claim rests on.

7.4 Switch rate

Median switch rate 0.077 (range 0.042–0.200). Criterion (17) was met by 5 of 13 participants; median η = 0.645, with 3 participants above 1.0. Pooled across participants η = 0.419, which passes — pooling is inappropriate for a design whose unit is the individual. Reinforcement rate met the criterion for 13 of 13 (η = 0.311–0.537); p̂A for 13 of 13 (median 0.062).

Discriminating participants by held-out state gave AUC 0.832 for (p̂A, r̂, ℓ) at 80 transitions, against 0.810 with switch rate substituted for ℓ, 0.795 for all four and 0.792 for (p̂A, r̂). Under (19) with η* = 0.5 the three-coordinate state requires bins of 5–12 responses (median 9), all within the design's ceiling of 800/40 = 20.

7.5 Contingency against color

Per participant, (16) was computed for pairs sharing a contingency but differing in color, and for pairs sharing a color but differing in contingency. The first exceeded the second for 10 of 13 participants; median difference +0.069; Wilcoxon W = 16, p = 0.040; sign test p = 0.092. Pooled medians across 52 comparisons: 0.746 and 0.603.

8Limitations

8.1 Cell size. 36 transitions per cell makes (16) unusable and is the binding constraint on §7.3 and §7.5. Raising the reversal stages by 50% gives 1,200 responses per cell, 40 transitions at B = 30, and raises the ceiling in (19) from 20 to 30, at roughly eight minutes more per session.

8.2 Sample. N = 13 for the between-participant claim in §7.5, where the sign test does not reach conventional significance.

8.3 Schedule richness. Obtained reinforcement was 4.08 per 10-response bin against a design floor of 2.0. Switch rate fell as the schedule was corrected across pilots — 0.082, 0.086, 0.058 — consistent with staying becoming more profitable than leaving under (1)–(3) as parameterized. Whether the low switch rates of §7.4 are a property of participants or of the parameterization is not separable from these data.

8.4 Perturbations. Each perturbation lasts 8 responses against B = 10, so no bin lies wholly within one. Recovery after offset is cleanly binned; the perturbation itself is not. Perturbation blocks appear in Figure 1 but are not analyzed here.

8.5 Provenance. Three participants came from a final pilot on the same build; replaying their response sequences through the current code reproduces their reinforcement outcomes exactly, so no schedule difference separates them. Two of those sessions were reconstructed from participant-supplied exports after an upload failure, verified against the partial rows already stored and agreeing on all 49 logged columns.
