Technical report · final dataset · 12 September 2026

Per-individual transition operators: what 23 single subjects show

96,980 responses. Every subject's operator predicts that subject's own later behavior, and for 19 of 23 it predicts every re-exposure to a context they had already met. Whether an operator is organized by the contingency or by the signal cannot be determined for any individual in this dataset.

Summary

Three questions, answered per subject rather than pooled.

Can an operator be recovered for an individual? Yes, for 23 of 23. Every subject's fitted operator beat that same subject's persistence baseline on temporally held-out data (median skill 0.505, minimum +0.305).

Does it hold when the context returns? For 19 of 23 subjects, an operator fitted to the first exposure of a context predicted the second exposure with positive skill in all four of that subject's contexts. 21 of 23 managed at least three of four. No subject was majority negative.

Is the operator organized by contingency or by signal? Undetermined. Each subject provides four matched comparisons; only 1 of 23 had all four favor the contingency and only 1 had all four favor the color. The remaining 21 were mixed, with individual comparisons frequently spanning both directions. This is a resolution limit of the similarity measure at 36 transitions per estimate, not a finding about the subjects.

1What the comparison asks

Green and blue each signal which panel pays better, and the ABAB reversals swap that mapping halfway through. Each subject therefore meets four contexts: green with A rich, green with B rich, blue with A rich, blue with B rich.

Two kinds of pair can be formed from these. A same-contingency pair holds the payoff structure fixed and changes the background — green with A rich against blue with A rich. A same-signal pair holds the background fixed and reverses the payoff — green with A rich against green with B rich. The design supplies four of each per subject, matched on stage pair, so the two kinds are equally separated in time.

If a subject's operator is more similar within same-contingency pairs, their moment-to-moment dynamics are organized around which alternative pays: the same reinforcement relation produces the same pattern of change regardless of what the screen looks like. If more similar within same-signal pairs, the dynamics are organized around the stimulus: the subject has acquired a color-specific way of responding that survives the payoff reversing underneath it.

2Allocation, subject by subject

3Each subject's operator predicts that subject's own re-exposures

Each of a subject's four contexts is presented twice, 800 responses apart. Fitting on the first presentation and predicting the second gives four within-subject tests per person.

Nineteen subjects are green — every context they met replicated. Two are amber. Two are red and deserve naming: 6a19b144 (2 of 4, median −0.027) and 699b19eb (2 of 4, median −0.332, with one context at −2.888). Both nonetheless had positive overall held-out skill (+0.305 and +0.316), so their operators carry something; it simply does not transfer across the 800-response gap in every context.

4Contingency against signal: not resolvable per subject

One subject shows four of four (5dbcdf38, median +0.087) and one shows none (650f06d2, median −0.339). The other 21 are mixed, and the within-subject ranges are wide: 6931bc7a's four pairs span −0.136 to +0.979, 69807807's span −0.790 to +0.880.

This is a measurement limit, not a null result about behavior. Two independent estimates of the same operator at 36 transitions have a similarity of 0.735 with a 5th–95th range of [0.087, 0.982]. A single pair therefore tells you almost nothing, and four pairs per subject cannot average that away. The design would need roughly 1,200 responses per cell rather than 800 to make this question answerable per subject.

Pooling across subjects does not rescue it either: 16 of 23 subjects had a positive median difference, which is the sort of majority that invites a group test, but no individual result underlies it.

6What these data support

Supported per subject. A linear transition operator can be estimated for an individual from roughly 375 transitions and predicts that individual's held-out behavior better than persistence. This held for every subject tested, with the weakest at +0.305. For 19 of 23, the operator also predicted every re-exposure to a context met 800 responses earlier.

Not supported. That an individual's operator is organized around the reinforcement contingency rather than the signal announcing it. One subject of 23 shows that pattern consistently, which is what chance would produce.

Out of reach with this dataset. The eigenvector similarity measure at 36 transitions per estimate. This is fixed by session length, not sample size; no number of additional subjects would change it. A future design wanting this question answered needs about 50% longer reversal stages.

6.1 Other limits

Switch rate cleared the noise criterion for 7 of 23 subjects and was excluded from the state; reinforcement rate cleared it for 22 of 23. Obtained reinforcement was 4.08 per ten-response bin against a design floor of 2.0, and switch rate fell across successive schedule corrections (0.082, 0.086, 0.058), consistent with staying having become more profitable than leaving; whether the low switch rates are a property of subjects or of the parameterization is not separable here. Two subjects lost trailing perturbation blocks to the session time guard, which is why the weakest design cell holds 19 rather than 72 transitions. Perturbations last 8 responses against a 10-response bin and are not analyzed.
